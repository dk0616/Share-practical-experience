When our product team first discussed launching a private-label enterprise wireless access point, the conversation revolved around manufacturing costs. Like many networking companies in Europe, we had years of experience selling enterprise connectivity solutions, managed network services, and business broadband. We understood customer requirements, channel development, and technical support. What we did not have was experience designing wireless hardware.

At the beginning, our assumption was simple: find a factory that could manufacture our product according to our specifications. We spent weeks contacting suppliers across different regions, comparing quotations and production capacity. Most discussions focused on pricing, minimum order quantities, and delivery schedules. Everything looked similar until we started asking technical questions. That was when we realized manufacturing was only one small part of the entire process.

Our company, based in the United Kingdom, had decided to expand beyond service offerings by introducing a branded WiFi 6 ceiling access point for business customers. Demand for managed WiFi services had grown steadily among hotels, office buildings, retail chains, and educational institutions. Customers increasingly preferred purchasing networking infrastructure together with long-term technical support instead of sourcing hardware separately. Building our own product line therefore became a logical step in strengthening our business model.

However, after conducting an internal assessment, we identified several critical gaps. We had software developers, network engineers, and customer support specialists, but no RF engineers, no hardware design team, and no experience with antenna tuning, EMC validation, or wireless certification. Developing an enterprise-grade access point from the ground up would likely require more than a year of engineering work before production could even begin. The investment and technical risk were difficult to justify for a company's first hardware product.

Everything changed when we began evaluating [Chinese wireless access point manufacturers](https://wifioem.com/why-global-brands-source-from-chinese-wireless-access-point-manufacturers/)  from a different perspective. Instead of asking who could manufacture our design, we started asking who could help us build a complete product. That shift completely changed the supplier selection process.

After several rounds of technical discussions, we decided to establish an OEM partnership with COMFAST. The project centered around an enterprise WiFi 6 ceiling access point designed for business environments. Unlike some manufacturers that limited their role to production, the COMFAST engineering team became involved from the earliest planning stage. Before discussing production schedules, they wanted to understand our customers, deployment environments, management platform, and long-term product roadmap.

Our objective was clear. We needed an enterprise-grade WiFi 6 access point capable of supporting hotels, office buildings, schools, and retail stores while integrating with our existing network management services. The device needed Power over Ethernet support, centralized controller compatibility, stable roaming performance, and reliable operation under continuous business workloads. At the same time, it had to carry our own brand identity and remain compatible with our future product strategy.

The engineering collaboration quickly extended beyond manufacturing. COMFAST supported PCB production, SMT assembly, RF optimisation, thermal analysis, product ageing tests, EMC preparation, and manufacturing validation. During weekly online meetings, their RF engineers explained design decisions that helped our own software team better understand how hardware behaviour affected wireless performance. These conversations shortened development time because potential issues were addressed before production rather than after deployment.

The project began with an engineering validation batch of 80 units, followed by a pilot deployment of 500 devices installed across several customer environments. These included boutique hotels, serviced offices, education facilities, and small retail chains. The pilot phase generated valuable operational feedback, particularly regarding roaming behaviour, PoE compatibility, controller management, and firmware optimisation. Rather than treating these observations as customer complaints, both engineering teams reviewed the deployment results together and incorporated improvements before mass production.

Once validation was completed, the first manufacturing order increased to approximately 6,000 units. Today, annual procurement exceeds 30,000 devices, with the access points deployed across a wide variety of commercial environments throughout the United Kingdom. The hardware has become an integral part of our managed networking portfolio rather than simply another product catalogue item.

One aspect that impressed our management team most was the maturity of the engineering support process. COMFAST provided documentation for CE preparation, coordinated production milestones, assisted with RF optimisation, and maintained regular technical communication throughout manufacturing. This level of collaboration reduced uncertainty during every stage of the project. We initially expected to manage a supplier relationship. Instead, we developed an engineering partnership.

Looking back, our biggest misconception was believing that hardware manufacturing was mainly about production capacity. In reality, successful enterprise networking products require coordinated expertise across RF engineering, certification, manufacturing, quality control, firmware integration, and supply chain management. Production is only one stage of a much larger engineering process.

Many companies still associate Chinese manufacturers primarily with competitive pricing. Cost certainly remains important, but our experience demonstrated that price was never the decisive factor. What ultimately influenced our decision was engineering capability, responsiveness, manufacturing consistency, and the ability to solve technical problems collaboratively. Those qualities proved far more valuable than achieving the lowest quotation.

Today, when other networking companies ask why we chose to manufacture in China, my answer is very different from what it would have been several years ago. We did not select a supplier because we needed a factory. We selected a partner because we needed engineering knowledge, manufacturing experience, and a reliable supply chain working together toward the same objective. That, more than anything else, explains why so many global networking brands continue building long-term relationships with Chinese wireless access point manufacturers.
