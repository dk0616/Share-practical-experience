A German home network dealer began seeing a new type of customer request after remote work became more common. These customers were not asking for the fastest router on the shelf. They were asking why their home VPN setup felt messy. In one typical household, one adult needed to access an overseas company system, another joined video meetings and used cloud storage throughout the day, while the same home also had a smart TV, game console, phones, tablets, and several ordinary family devices online.

The problem was not that VPN apps were useless. They worked well enough on individual laptops and phones. The difficulty came from managing every device separately. The work laptop used one VPN setting, the personal phone used another, and the smart TV or game console could not be configured easily. When cloud file synchronization started during a video meeting, the family sometimes felt that the VPN connection affected daily work stability. The customer did not want a technical lecture. They wanted a simpler way to manage the home network.

The dealer selected COMFAST CF-WR633AX V2 for a first batch of 130 units aimed at households with frequent VPN use. COMFAST engineers helped the dealer build a practical selection table for family VPN router scenarios. Instead of presenting VPN as an abstract privacy feature, the table grouped users by real behavior: remote workers, families using overseas services, households with smart TVs and consoles, and users who wanted fewer device-by-device settings.

This training changed how the dealer explained a [VPN router for families](https://wifioem.com/why-more-families-are-using-vpn-routers/). The sales team learned to ask whether customers used VPN every day, whether the TV or console needed network access through the same route, whether video meetings were affected by file uploads, and whether some devices should stay on a normal connection while others used VPN. These questions made the router recommendation easier to understand.

COMFAST engineers also provided guidance on device grouping and traffic separation. In many homes, not every device needs to use VPN all the time. A work laptop may need VPN for company systems, while a game console or smart TV may need a different setup depending on the household’s habits. Helping customers understand this difference reduced confusion and made the installation process smoother.

After the first deployments, the dealer reported fewer repeated questions about how to configure VPN on every device. Customers also understood that the router was not simply making the internet faster. It was helping the family manage connected devices in a cleaner way. For many households, that was the real value: fewer settings, fewer conflicting apps, and a more organized home network.

The lesson was practical. A VPN router is not only for technical users. It can be useful for ordinary families when work, entertainment, and multiple devices share the same home network every day.
