I used to believe that a wireless network problem could always be solved by adding another device.

That belief changed after managing the network upgrade of a manufacturing company in southern Poland.

The company had around 120 employees working between administration offices, engineering departments, meeting rooms, and a production planning area. The business was not large enough to have a dedicated network team, but its daily operations depended heavily on stable connectivity.

Employees used cloud-based ERP software, shared engineering documents, video meetings, and wireless barcode devices connected to internal systems.

The internet connection was stable.

The problem was the wireless network inside the building.

When More Devices Created More Problems

The original WiFi system had developed naturally over several years.

Whenever employees reported weak signals, another consumer router or extender was purchased. Each decision seemed reasonable at that moment.

However, after several years, nobody had a complete picture of the network.

Some devices used different configurations. Some areas had overlapping signals. Employees walking from meeting rooms to offices often had to manually reconnect because their laptops stayed attached to weaker access points.

The company did not have a complete WiFi failure. It had a network that was no longer suitable for its current size.

Many growing companies experience the same situation. They increase the number of devices without redesigning the network structure. Understanding the Real Requirements

Before replacing equipment, the company contacted COMFAST for technical evaluation.

The first step was not selecting a product model.

The engineers asked about the building layout, employee locations, connected devices, business applications, and future expansion plans.

They inspected several areas:

open office areas
conference rooms
engineering departments
production planning spaces
warehouse entrances

The results showed that different areas had different requirements.

The meeting rooms needed higher client capacity because many visitors connected at the same time.

The engineering department needed stable access for large files and cloud applications.

The warehouse area required careful AP placement because machinery and metal structures affected wireless signals.

Designing a Business WiFi System

The final design replaced independent routers with centrally managed wireless access points.

Ceiling-mounted WiFi 6 access points were installed in office areas. PoE switches supplied power and network connection through a single cable, making installation cleaner and easier to maintain.

The company also separated network access.

Employees used the internal network.

Visitors received a guest network.

Operational devices were placed in dedicated segments.

This structure improved management and reduced unnecessary traffic between different users.

The access points were managed through a centralized system, allowing administrators to monitor connected clients, check device status, and identify abnormal situations.

Technical Support Beyond Equipment Delivery

One important part of the project was training.

The company’s IT administrator was familiar with basic networking but had limited experience managing multiple professional access points.

COMFAST engineers provided remote guidance during installation.

The training covered:

AP configuration
SSID management
wireless channel adjustment
client monitoring
firmware updates
common troubleshooting methods

This allowed the company to maintain the network independently instead of depending on external support for every adjustment.

The Result After Deployment

The improvement was most noticeable during busy working hours.

Previously, employees in meeting rooms often experienced unstable video conferences when many devices connected simultaneously.

After the upgrade, meetings became more consistent.

Engineers working with cloud platforms experienced fewer interruptions.

The warehouse team also reported more reliable wireless connections when using mobile devices.

The company did not achieve this result because it purchased the most expensive equipment.

It achieved it because the network design matched the actual working environment.

For small and medium businesses, WiFi is often treated as background infrastructure until problems appear. However, as companies add more employees, more applications, and more connected devices, wireless planning becomes part of daily business operation.

A good network should not require constant attention from employees.

It should simply allow them to work.
