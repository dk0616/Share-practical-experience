I have worked on many wireless network projects, but retail environments always bring a different type of challenge. Unlike a factory or office, a coffee shop changes throughout the day. The number of connected users rises during busy hours, customers move around with mobile devices, and employees depend on stable connectivity for daily operations.

This article shares an experience from a coffee chain project in Brazil where our team helped improve wireless coverage across multiple stores.

The customer operated several coffee shops in different commercial areas. Each location had a similar requirement: provide reliable WiFi for customers while keeping internal business devices connected.

Before the upgrade, each store used a basic wireless router placed near the service counter. It worked when the number of users was low, but problems appeared during peak periods.

The store managers noticed several issues:

Customers near the entrance sometimes had weak signals.
Employees reported unstable connections for payment terminals and management systems.
Adding new devices required manual configuration.
Different stores had different network settings.

The customer contacted a local network integrator to redesign the wireless system.

The first step was understanding the environment. A coffee shop may not have a large area, but walls, furniture, and customer density influence wireless performance.

After evaluating the requirements, the team selected ceiling-mounted wireless access points as the main solution.

Unlike desktop routers, ceiling access points are designed to provide more balanced coverage from a higher installation position. They can also be connected through PoE, allowing power and network data to travel through the same cable.

During deployment, engineers focused on several details:

Selecting installation positions away from interference sources
Planning coverage areas
Separating customer and employee networks
Testing connection stability during busy hours

The local installation team received technical guidance about device configuration and basic troubleshooting.

For a multi-store business, consistency was important. Store number one and store number ten should follow the same network structure.

After completing the project, the coffee chain achieved a more organized wireless environment.

Employees no longer needed to restart equipment frequently during busy periods. Store managers had fewer complaints about unstable customer WiFi, and new locations could follow the same deployment process.

This project showed that wireless coverage is not simply about adding stronger signals. Good network design requires understanding how people use the space.

For businesses planning similar upgrades, reviewing  [commercial WiFi installation practices](https://wifioem.com/what-is-a-ceiling-wireless-access-point/)  before purchasing equipment can help avoid many common problems.

A ceiling access point is only one part of the solution. The final result depends on installation planning, network configuration, and long-term maintenance.
