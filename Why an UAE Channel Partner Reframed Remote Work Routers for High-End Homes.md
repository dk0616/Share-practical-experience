In the UAE high-end residential market, customers rarely complain that their broadband package is too small. Many homes already have fast internet service, multiple entertainment devices, smart home systems, and several rooms designed for work, leisure, and guests. Yet one local channel partner noticed a repeating problem in large homes: the internet speed looked impressive on paper, but remote work still became unstable during busy hours.

The issue appeared most clearly in households where two adults worked remotely at the same time. One person might be on a video meeting in a study room, while another connected to a company VPN from a bedroom office. At the same time, other family members streamed 4K video, used a game console, checked security cameras, or controlled smart home devices. The broadband line itself was not the only factor. The router had to manage many different kinds of traffic without letting one activity disturb another.

The channel partner selected COMFAST CF-WR673BE for a first batch of 100 units aimed at high-end residential remote work upgrades. COMFAST engineers helped the partner create a WiFi 7 upgrade plan that focused on real usage rather than speed numbers alone. The training covered VPN behavior, video meeting stability, cloud file synchronization, remote room testing, and device grouping for busy households. This helped the sales team explain why a  [router for remote work](https://wifioem.com/best-routers-for-remote-work-in-2026/ )  should be judged by stability, coverage, latency, and multi-device handling, not only by the maximum speed printed on a box.

The partner also changed its customer conversation. Instead of asking only how fast the broadband plan was, the team began asking where meetings happened, how many people worked from home, whether VPN was used daily, which rooms had weak signal, and whether smart cameras or 4K streaming ran during working hours. These questions made the router recommendation feel more practical. Customers could understand that a home office in a distant room has different requirements from a phone used near the main router.

COMFAST engineers also provided guidance for far-room testing. In large UAE homes, distance and wall structure can create a misleading situation: the living room test looks strong, but the bedroom office performs poorly during a call. The partner learned to test where work actually happens, not only beside the router. This small change helped reduce disputes after installation because customers could see the difference between general coverage and usable office performance.

After the first deployments, the channel partner found that high-end customers were more willing to accept a long-term upgrade plan when it was explained through their daily routines. A video call dropping during a client meeting is more serious than a slow entertainment download. A VPN session freezing during document approval can interrupt real work. By connecting the router choice to these specific situations, the partner moved away from generic product selling and toward network planning for modern homes.

The result was not described as a dramatic speed change. It was described as fewer interruptions, clearer expectations, and better matching between equipment and household workload. For remote work homes, that is often the difference that matters most.
